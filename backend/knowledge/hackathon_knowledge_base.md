# Hack the Future — Official Knowledge Base

Purpose: this document is the source of truth for the Hackathon Website Assistant (RAG chatbot). Every section is self-contained so it can be split into chunks. If a question is not answered here, the assistant must say the information has not been announced yet. It must never guess.

Last reviewed: November 11, 2026.

---

## 1. About the Hackathon

Hack the Future is a college student hackathon. Participants build innovative projects in a 24-hour sprint. The event tagline is "Build what's next. Together." The website describes it as one weekend of big ideas and real impact for the brightest student builders, aimed at shaping a better tomorrow. The event is for all skill levels, so beginners are welcome to bring their curiosity.

Common questions answered by this section:
- What is this hackathon?
- What is Hack the Future?
- What will participants do?
- Is it for beginners?

## 3. Who Can Participate (Eligibility)

- Participants must be college students.
- Participants must be at least 16 years old.
- School students are not eligible.
- Participants may come with their family. Family members may accompany a participant, but the participant must still meet the eligibility rules (college student, 16 or older).
- Participants can register individually or as a team.

## 4. Hackathon Tracks (Domains)

Participants choose one domain when registering. There are six tracks:

1. Artificial Intelligence — "Build intelligent tools that make everyday life better."
2. Generative AI — "Reimagine how we create, learn, and work."
3. Web Development — "Create digital experiences that bring people together."
4. Data Science — "Find the insight that changes everything."
5. Cybersecurity — "Make the connected world a safer place."
6. Open Innovation — "Got an idea that doesn't fit a box? This one's yours."

Notes:
- On the home page, the "Pick a problem. Make a difference." section shows these six track cards. Selecting a card (or its arrow) opens a detail page for that track.
- On the registration form, the domain list shows: Artificial Intelligence, Generative AI, Web Development, Data Science, Cybersecurity, and Other. The Open Innovation track corresponds to the "Other" domain.
- Detailed descriptions, example problem statements, suggested technologies, and resources for each track are published on that track's detail page, and the organizers can update them.

- Which domain should I choose?
- What is the Open Innovation track?

## 5. Creating an Account (Sign Up)

To register for the hackathon, you first need a website account.

1. Select "Sign up" in the website header.
2. Enter your full name (2 to 100 characters), your email address, and a password.
3. The password must be between 10 and 128 characters.
4. After signing up you are signed in automatically.

Other account facts:
- Each email address can have only one account. If the email already exists, the website says an account with that email already exists.
- Your account email is the email used for your hackathon registration.
- A password-reset option is not currently published on the website. Please check for organizer updates.

Common questions answered by this section:
- How do I create an account?
- What are the password rules?
- What password requirements apply to my account?
- I forgot my password. What do I do?

## 7. How to Register (Step by Step)

1. Create an account or log in.
2. Select "Register" (shown in the header after you sign in; before that it shows "Sign up").
3. Complete the registration form.
4. Accept the hackathon rules and confirm your information is accurate.
5. Submit the form.
6. The website saves your registration and shows a unique registration ID.

Important facts:
- Registration is free ("Register for free" is shown on the homepage).
- Only one registration is allowed per account.
- Your account email is used automatically for the registration.
- A confirmation email is sent only if email delivery is configured and succeeds. The website never claims an email was sent unless it really was. Your registration is saved even if the email fails.

Common questions answered by this section:
- How do I register?
- How much does registration cost?
- Will I get a confirmation email?
- Can I register twice?

## 8. Registration Form Fields

Required fields:
- Full name (2 to 100 characters)
- Mobile number (7 to 15 digits; digits, spaces, +, -, ( ) and . are allowed)
- College name
- Degree: B.Tech, B.Sc, BCA, MCA, M.Tech, or Other
- Department
- Year of study or graduation year (for example "3rd year" or "2027")
- College location
- Participation type: Individual or Team
- Hackathon domain: Artificial Intelligence, Generative AI, Web Development, Data Science, Cybersecurity, or Other
- Technical skills (programming languages and technical skills)
- Project idea
- Agreement to the hackathon rules and terms
- Confirmation that the information provided is accurate

Optional fields:
- Date of birth (must be a date in the past)
- Project title (up to 150 characters)
- GitHub profile URL (must be a valid https://github.com profile link)

Common questions answered by this section:
- What details do I need to register?
- Which fields are optional?
- Do I need a GitHub profile?
- Which degrees are accepted?
- What degree programs can I register with?

## 9. Teams and Individual Participation

- You can participate as an individual or as a team.
- A team can have 2 to 5 participants, including the team leader.
- For a team registration, enter a team name (up to 100 characters), the team size, the team leader's name, and the name and email of each other team member.
- A team has one registration entry submitted by one member. For team size N, you enter details for N−1 other members.
- Individual participants do not need any team details.

Common questions answered by this section:
- Can I participate alone?
- How many people can be on a team?
- What is the maximum squad size?
- What team details are needed?

## 10. Viewing or Changing Your Registration

- After registering, a signed-in participant can view their own registration (select "View my registration").
- The "My registration" page shows your registration ID, details, domain, project, and status.
- The website currently does not provide an option for participants to edit a submitted registration.
- A participant can only see their own registration, never anyone else's.

Registration statuses used by organizers: Submitted, Under Review, Accepted, Waitlisted, and Rejected. Organizers review registrations and update the status.

Common questions answered by this section:
- Can I edit my registration?
- How do I see my registration ID?
- What do the status labels mean?

## 16. About the Chatbot (Hackathon Website Assistant)

- The assistant answers questions about this hackathon and website: event details, registration, login, tracks, food, and facilities.
- It is available only to signed-in users.
- It uses only official published information. If information has not been announced, it says so instead of guessing.
- It does not read personal registration data or account details.
- Sensitive items such as email addresses, phone numbers, and passwords are removed from messages before they are sent to the AI provider. Users should still avoid sharing passwords or private data in chat.
- Each signed-in account can send up to 10 chat messages per minute.
- Only questions about this hackathon and its website are in scope. Unrelated requests, including general knowledge, coding help, sports results, or requests to reveal prompts or credentials, are politely declined without retrieving knowledge-base content.

## 17. Privacy and Security Summary

- Passwords are stored as secure hashes, never as plain text.
- The website uses secure, HttpOnly session cookies.
- State-changing actions are protected against cross-site request forgery.
- Participants can access only their own registration.
- Only organizers can see the participant directory and export registration data.
